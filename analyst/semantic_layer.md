# Semantic layer: what the clean data means

The clean layer is schema `clean` in olist.duckdb: one Brazilian marketplace (Olist), orders from September 2016 to October 2018, 99,441 orders. Figures come from it; the raw CSVs are never read directly.

## Words and their meaning here
- **Customer**: a person, `customer_unique_id` on `clean.orders` (96,096 people). Count customers on that column, never on orders or on `customer_id`. About 3% of people ordered more than once ("repeat buyers": `clean.customers.n_orders > 1`).
- **Late**: delivered after the promised date: `is_late` on `clean.orders` (true, false, or NULL when the order was not delivered or has no delivery date). Share of late orders = `round(100.0 * avg(CASE WHEN is_late THEN 1 ELSE 0 END), 2) AS late_share_pct` over delivered orders with `is_late IS NOT NULL`. About 8% of delivered orders are late. A question about slow, delayed or poor delivery means late in this sense (`is_late`), not a long `delivery_days`; say so as a chosen definition. Use `delivery_days` only when the question asks about days or how long delivery takes.
- **Delivery time**: `delivery_days` = calendar days from purchase to delivery. `days_vs_promise` = calendar days delivered minus promised (positive = late, negative = early). Both NULL unless delivered with a date.
- **Unhappy customer / bad review**: `review_score` 1 or 2 (scores run 1 to 5). "Happy" = 4 or 5. About 41% of reviews have a text; the rest only a score.
- **Revenue / sales**: `order_payment_value` on `clean.orders` (what the buyer paid, items plus freight); `items_value` is the price of the items alone; `freight_value` the shipping. Money figures (revenue, sales, order value) leave out canceled and unavailable orders: `status NOT IN ('canceled', 'unavailable')` (1,234 orders, about 270 thousand in payment value); say so as an assumption. There is no cost, margin or profit anywhere in the data.
- **Period**: data runs from 2016-09 to 2018-10. The complete months are 2017-01 to 2018-08: 2018-09 has 16 orders and 2018-10 has 4; 2016 holds only 329 orders (2016-09: 4, 2016-10: 324, 2016-12: 1, none in November). A question "per year" is really 2017 vs 2018; say so as an assumption. Use `purchase_ts` for every date question unless the question names delivery. "Last month", "this year" or "recently" point at today, which lies after the data: say that the data ends in October 2018 (cannot_answer, offer the last complete month instead) rather than pick a month.
- **Comparison window (year on year)**: every comparison of 2017 with 2018 (growth, change, "vs", "compared to", per state or per category) uses January to August of both years, `month(purchase_ts) BETWEEN 1 AND 8`, the same complete months on both sides, and says so as an assumption. A plain figure for one year ("how many orders in 2018?") takes the whole year. Any comparison with 2016 says plainly that 2016 holds only 329 orders (mostly October), so growth from 2016 is not meaningful, and gives the figures with that warning.
- **State / location**: the buyer's state is `customer_state` on `clean.orders` (the address of that order; a person can order from two states). For orders and revenue by region use `clean.orders.customer_state`, not `clean.customers`. Sellers have `seller_state` on `clean.sellers`.

## Ambiguous words (name the reading you take as an assumption, or ask)
- "customers" can mean people (`customer_unique_id`) or orders; take people unless the question counts orders.
- "sales" can mean number of orders, number of items, or payment value; take payment value for "revenue", order count for "how many".
- "delivery time" can mean purchase to delivery (`delivery_days`) or the delay against the promise (`days_vs_promise`); take `delivery_days`.
- "category" is per item, not per order: an order can hold items of several categories; count or sum on `clean.order_items` / `p_items`.
- "payment type" is per payment, not per order: 2,246 orders mix types (for example card plus voucher). Payment questions read `clean.payments` / `p_payments`; there is no payment type column on orders. An order with two voucher rows is still one order.
- "last year" / "this year": the data ends in October 2018; say which year you take.
- "which ... most" / "top" without a number (states, categories, sellers): return every group, sorted, so the owner sees the whole ranking in the chart; name the top ones in the text.
- A question for one figure (the share of one payment type, one count, one average) returns one row with that figure, plus its parts if useful (the voucher value and the total), never a breakdown per group; a breakdown is for questions that say "per", "by", "which" or "top".

## Grain rule (fan-out)
Order-level columns (`order_payment_value`, `items_value`, `freight_value`, `delivery_days`, `review_score`, `is_late`, `n_items`) live on `clean.orders` / `p_orders`. Sum or average them from that table alone. Join `clean.order_items` / `p_items` only for item-level figures (price per item, category, seller) and `clean.payments` / `p_payments` only for payment-level figures (type, installments). Joining items or payments onto orders and then summing an order column counts an order once per item or payment: wrong. For "revenue by category" sum `price` on the items table, not `order_payment_value`.

## Population and cohort (when the question is about a group of orders)
Every narrowing of the orders (a status, a period, a state, late or not, a category, a score band), also a narrowing inside an aggregate, lives in `population.sql` (one SELECT on clean returning `order_id`, optionally `cohort`) or in its `cohort` column, never in the figures SQL. The figures SQL then reads `p_orders`, `p_items`, `p_payments`, `p_reviews` (the clean tables cut to that population, each row carrying `cohort`) and has no WHERE or HAVING. A comparison (late vs on time, 2017 vs 2018, one state vs the rest) is one population with a `cohort` column, one cohort per order; the figures `GROUP BY cohort`. Cohort labels are short snake_case words: `late` / `on_time`, `2017` / `2018`, `10_or_more` / `fewer`. Growth or change between two cohorts: pivot in a CTE to one row with the figure per cohort, the difference and the growth as a `_pct` column (per state or category: one row per group with the same columns). Customer texts for a why-question are searched in `p_reviews` of the same population, so the quotes and the figures describe the same orders.

## Tables
- `clean.orders` (one row per order): `order_id`, `customer_unique_id`, `customer_zip_prefix`, `customer_city`, `customer_state`, `status` (delivered, shipped, canceled, unavailable, invoiced, processing, created, approved; delivery figures use `status = 'delivered'`), `purchase_ts`, `approved_ts`, `carrier_ts`, `delivered_ts` (empty for about 3% of delivered orders), `estimated_ts` (the promise), `delivery_days`, `days_vs_promise`, `is_late`, `review_score` (from the latest review of the order, NULL without a review), `n_items`, `n_sellers`, `items_value`, `freight_value`, `order_payment_value`, `n_payment_types`, `distance_km` (buyer to seller, averaged over the order's sellers).
- `clean.order_items` (one row per item in an order): `order_id`, `order_item_id`, `product_id`, `seller_id`, `shipping_limit_ts`, `price`, `freight_value`, `category` (Portuguese), `category_english` (use this one in answers).
- `clean.payments` (one row per payment): `order_id`, `payment_sequential`, `payment_type` (credit_card, boleto, voucher, debit_card, not_defined), `installments`, `payment_value`.
- `clean.reviews` (one row per order, the latest review): `review_id`, `order_id`, `score`, `title`, `text` (Portuguese, NULL for 59%), `created_ts`, `answered_ts`, `embedding` (for the review search; never select it).
- `clean.customers` (one row per person): `customer_unique_id`, `n_orders`, `first_order_ts`, `last_order_ts`, and the location of the person's latest order.
- `clean.sellers` (one row per seller): `seller_id`, `seller_zip_prefix`, `seller_city`, `seller_state`.

## Not in the data
Customer names, ages, gender, income; marketing spend, website visits, stock, returns, refunds, costs, margins, profit; reasons for cancellation; anything before September 2016 or after October 2018. Say so and offer what is there.

## DuckDB notes
`days` is a reserved word: name a day count `delivery_days`. A top-level UNION is not allowed: compare groups with a `cohort` column or with CTEs. Compute every share, ratio and difference in SQL with a clear column name. Every share, rate or growth is a percentage column whose name ends in `_pct`, already multiplied by 100 and rounded to 2 decimals: `round(100.0 * ..., 2) AS late_share_pct`, `round(100.0 * (r2018 / r2017 - 1), 2) AS growth_pct`. Never return a fraction (0.08) for a percentage; the page shows a `_pct` column with a % sign.
